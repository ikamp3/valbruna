# Austrian resident, 50% Czech s.r.o. shareholder and jednatel: structure, tax and citizenship analysis

As of 25 Nov 2026. **Research limit:** the session network blocked RIS, BMF/findok, SVS, MA 35, Czech MF and the law-firm pages (DNS failure). Only web-search snippets were readable. Statutory wording (§ 10 StbG, GSVG § 2 and § 25, treaty Arts 10, 15, 16 and 22) was **not** read. Every item is tagged:
**[V]** seen in a search result · **[U]** my own legal knowledge, not verified this session · **[A]** assumption.
This is not a substitute for a signed opinion from an Austrian and a Czech adviser.

## 1. Corrections to the ChatGPT preliminary structure

1. **EUR 2,200/month does not reach the citizenship threshold.** Net is roughly EUR 1,267/month under the Czech-taxation model (EUR 1,551 if only Austria taxes). The EUR 1,560–1,610 estimate only works if Czech tax is ignored and Austria taxes alone.
2. **Czech tax is real from 1 Jan 2026.** The 15% final withholding tax on non-resident statutory-body members was replaced by monthly payroll advance tax at 15%/23%. A Czech return is required above 36× the average wage [V: KPMG, EY, Grant Thornton, Forvis Mazars, PKF Apogeo; see links]. The Czech company therefore needs Czech payroll registration.
3. **Dividends may add SVS contributions.** VwGH Ro 2023/08/0006 (2 July 2024) held that profit distributions to a GSVG-insured managing shareholder of a *chamber-member* GmbH fall into the GSVG contribution base [V via KPMG/Forvis]. Deloitte says this presupposes GSVG compulsory insurance. Whether it carries over to a Czech s.r.o. is unresolved. Do not assume dividends are contribution-free.
4. **The "50% ownership threshold" is not a clean rule.** The only "50% / blocking minority" material I found was German law and is not relevant. For Austria, a 50% holder in a 50/50 company can block every majority resolution, so I treat the Austrian GSVG/neue Selbständige classification as the likely outcome [U]. The ASVG employee route is unlikely.

## 3. Net-income model (Austrian GSVG, EUR, FX assumed 1 EUR = CZK 24.3 [A])

Austrian social-insurance rates 2026: PV 18.5%, KV 6.8%, Selbständigenvorsorge 1.53%, accident EUR 12.95/month, minimum base EUR 6,613.20, maximum base EUR 97,020 [V: WKO]. Total about 26.83% of gross plus EUR 155/yr. Austrian income tax uses the 2026 brackets as I know them: 0% to 13,539, then 20/30/40/48/50% [U, verify], with a 15% basic profit allowance up to EUR 33,000 profit [U, verify].

Reading: **remuneration beats dividends for me**, because each EUR of pay costs the other shareholder half of the deduction value. The marginal benefit is about +0.30 per EUR at 15% Czech tax and 26.83% SV. The other shareholder loses about EUR 600 per EUR 10,000 of pay at the 50/50 split, so the pay must be arm's length and unanimously approved. If SV applies to dividends, higher remuneration wins even more clearly. So "lowest possible pay" is not the optimum: **pay to the citizenship need, then distribute the rest.** Czech 21% CIT is final; I did not model profit retention versus distribution timing.

## 7. Corporate-tax risks

- **Austrian permanent establishment / place of effective management:** decisions taken by the jednatel from Vienna are a real risk. Czech residence tests by place of management and registered seat are also in play [U]. Mitigate with genuine Czech substance: the other shareholder or a Czech co-director takes real decisions, board minutes are kept, and strategic decisions are documented as Czech. Do not fabricate Czech management.
- **Transfer pricing:** pay must be arm's length and documented.
- **Dual residence:** the Austria–Czech treaty tie-breaker for companies applies [U].
- **Austrian Kommunalsteuer / DB / DZ** may apply to a >25% managing shareholder only through an Austrian permanent establishment [U]. Check.

## 8. Immigration

A Georgian long-term resident of Austria may own shares and be a jednatel of a Czech s.r.o. (corporate-law rights are independent of immigration status) [U]. Working remotely from Austria uses the Austrian residence permit, which must allow self-employment [U, check the card]. Short business trips to Czechia for meetings are generally not "work" needing a Czech permit [U]. Rights to perform actual work in Czechia as a third-country national need confirmation. Do this with Czech counsel.

## 9. Implementation plan (indicative; costs are my own estimates [A])

1. **Before signing:** get a binding SVS/ÖGK determination and an A1 for the Czech activity. Get a Czech opinion on whether non-resident jednatel pay triggers Czech social and health contributions, and a BMF EAS inquiry on Art 16 versus Art 15 and on the exemption method.
2. Shareholders' resolution approving the jednatel contract and fee (both shareholders). Czech payroll registration of the company for the non-resident. Annual Czech return where required.
3. Austria: SVS notification of the added activity. Report the income in the E1 and the foreign-income form (E1kv for dividends and exemption supplement). VAT: only for the consulting variant (reverse charge on B2B services to Czechia).
4. **Costs [A]:** one-time EUR 2,500–6,000 (legal and tax opinions, contracts, resolutions); monthly Czech payroll EUR 80–200; annual Austrian and Czech filings EUR 1,500–3,000. Timeline 4–8 weeks, plus the A1 and SVS wait.
5. **For MA 35 keep:** contract, resolutions, Czech payslips, bank statements, Austrian tax assessments (Einkommensteuerbescheide), SVS statements and the housing contract.

## 10. Executive summary

1. **Best structure:** B (jednatel contract) at a remuneration tied to the citizenship threshold, plus dividends for the remainder. Add D only with genuinely distinct services.
2. **Social-insurance regime:** Austrian GSVG/neue Selbständige, assuming the Austrian activity classification governs [U]. **Dividend SV is unresolved.**
3. **Minimum gross/month (Czech-tax model, 14× basis):** EUR 2,563 / 2,649 / 2,993 / 3,337 / 3,680 / 4,024 for housing costs of 0 / 400 / 600 / 800 / 1,000 / 1,200. If only Austria taxes: EUR 2,080 / 2,162 / 2,491 / 2,821 / 3,171 / 3,538.
4. **Recommended with buffer:** EUR 2,900–3,100 (housing up to 400), up to about EUR 4,300–4,500 at EUR 1,200 of housing.
5. **Expected personal net:** about EUR 1,630–1,780/month at EUR 3,000 gross (Czech-tax model: EUR 1,732).
6. **Company cost:** EUR 3,000/month, EUR 36,000/yr gross, about EUR 28,440 after CIT.
7. **Dividends:** distribute surplus profit; avoid dividends as the main income for citizenship.
8. **Principal risks:** Czech tax on the pay, Czech social/health contributions, SV on dividends, the 14× versus 12× basis, the Austrian place of management, and the other shareholder's veto.
9. **Resolve before signing:** the items in the plan above, plus the exact threshold wording in § 10 StbG.
10. **Confidence: 45/100.** The structure is directionally right, but three load-bearing questions are unverified: the treaty reading, the dividend SV and the exact § 10 StbG formula. Rerun this once the sources are reachable.
