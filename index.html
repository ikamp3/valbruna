    What changed

    The old warning signs are gone.

    Three years ago you could spot a scam by its broken English, its amateur website or its obviously fake document. Today scammers use AI too. Their emails are flawless, their websites look professional and their documents look real.

    Why now

    Your bank checks the name. Not the story behind it.

    Since October 2025, euro-area banks must check that a payee's name matches the IBAN before you send money. That catches typos and some fraud. It does not catch a fake investment, a fake relative or a fake job that uses a real bank account.

    If it has already happened

    Your money crossed a border. Your evidence can too.

    Around 90% of cross-border scam victims get nothing back, often because nobody reports it in the right place, in the right language, in time. The Recovery Desk turns what you have into a sealed, translated case file.

    1. ContainA step-by-step checklist for your bank, cards and accounts, in minutes.
    2. SealYour files, the timeline and our findings, sealed so anyone can verify nothing changed after you submitted them.
    3. TranslateInto the language of the country where the money went, checked by a person.
    4. RouteTo the right cybercrime unit, financial intelligence unit, regulator and receiving bank.

    Vallbruna prepares the evidence. You, your lawyer or your bank submit it. We do not provide legal representation.

    Irakli Ninua

    Irakli Ninua has spent more than fifteen years where capital moves fastest. He began in investment banking in Switzerland, building valuation models and M&A cases at Rausch Partners and Match-Point, and raising capital for funds and growth companies from Monaco to Kazakhstan. Since 2017 he has worked at the frontier of digital finance: as co-founder, CFO and head of business development of AnchorFinance in Dubai, as crypto treasurer of an Austrian start-up, and as an adviser on DeFi, digital assets and smart-contract audits.

Based in Zurich, he speaks ten languages, has lived in five countries and has travelled to 90. At Vallbruna he leads strategy, product and partnerships.

    Co-founder & Chairman

    Pavel Skrivanek

    Pavel Skrivanek started with nothing but languages. In 1992 he was a young economics graduate earning 4,500 crowns a month, translating for an agency that had stopped paying him. So he went out on his own: a one-man translation service, launched on a newspaper advert he could not yet afford. When the bank refused him a loan, he sold his small Prague flat and used the money to open five branches. All five were profitable within months.

    Three decades later, Skrivanek is one of the largest language-service groups in Central and Eastern Europe and ranks among the 50 largest language agencies in the world, according to CSA Research. The group operates in more than 15 countries, including the United States, works in over 100 languages and turns over more than half a billion crowns a year. Trained in foreign trade at the Prague University of Economics and Business, he brings Vallbruna the discipline of a founder who built from zero, and a global network for the multilingual work cross-border recovery depends on.
